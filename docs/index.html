How it works

From one jittery number to a toy

A contact on your avatar — an SPS or OGB socket, plug or touch zone — reports one number: how deep, 0 to 1. VRChat sends it over OSC only when it changes, and it jitters. Every motor runs that number through the chain below. The plots are live: they show the demo above.

1Input

What a motor listens to: zones on your avatar, a source you built from other contacts, or any OSC parameter. With several, the strongest one counts.

2Depth

How far in, right now — with a gain and, if you want, a curve.

3Speed

How fast the depth is changing. The stroke in and the stroke out are measured apart, each with its own gain.

4Punch

A short hit on top when a stroke is fast, ringing out in about a tenth of a second. Slow movement never triggers it.

5Combine

Depth and speed become one level: added, the larger of the two, or multiplied. Punch rides on top.

6Wake

A gate that opens on real activity, so a contact that only rests against you does nothing. In Sleep it counts strokes instead.

7Envelope

Rise and fall times round the level, so a toy never clicks on or off. Optional texture keeps a held level from feeling flat.

8Zero cut and output

Input back at zero means silence, at once. Then the chain's gain and the total strength scale the level, and the result is fitted into the range this toy can actually feel: remapped, not clipped.

Then the built-in Intiface engine sends it to the toy over Bluetooth. From the contact changing to the command for the toy we measure about 27 ms on our test bench: nothing in that path queues or waits for an answer.

What it does

Works out of the box. Every knob, if you want it.

Setting up

  • One file. No installer, no Python, no separate Intiface Central: the Buttplug server is inside. It can use your own Intiface Central instead.
  • Finds VRChat by itself. No IP or port to type.
  • Finds your toys by itself and takes them back when they reconnect. A chime tells you in VR when one connects or drops.
  • Updates in one click. It offers a new version at launch, and afterwards shows what changed.

Routing

  • Four modes — Combined, Separate and two of your own — decide which parts of your avatar drive which toy. Each avatar can remember its mode.
  • Touch and penetration apart. Every motor has a chain per kind of contact, merged by add, max or multiply.
  • SPS Sources builds a zone from any contact receivers, for avatars without the standard sockets.

Feel

  • Total output strength scales every toy at once and never touches your tuning.
  • Fitted to the toy. A gain and a min / max band per chain. Strokers such as a Solace Pro or an OSR2 follow the depth as position.
  • Sleep wakes only for real strokes. Off is instant silence.

From inside VRChat

  • Your avatar's menu gets a strength radial plus Mode, Off, Sleep and Test. They are unsynced parameters, so they cost 0 sync bits.
  • Output back to the avatar. Any motor's level can be sent as a parameter, to drive a glow, a blendshape or a meter.
  • “A toy is connected” as a parameter, for the whole rig or for one toy.

Safety

  • Anti-stuck. OSC only sends changes, so a contact that freezes — an avatar swap, a partner leaving — would hold a motor on. A value frozen part-way is cut after a second; one held at 100 % eases off after ten.
  • Off stops everything at once, from the app or from your avatar's menu.
  • Nothing leaves your PC except one question to GitHub at launch: is there a newer version?

Also in there

  • Statistics. Every session with its timeline, a when-you-play heatmap and a calendar. One switch turns it off and deletes it.
  • Tune without a partner. An input simulator, a live signal graph, and recording and replay of sessions.
  • OSC Inspector and Diagnostics for when an avatar or the link misbehaves.
  • Toys in SteamVR's device list, with battery. Never as trackers. Windows only for now.
  • Any colour, in a Vibrant or a Darker look. Every control explains itself when you rest the pointer on it.

Questions

Good to know

Why does Windows warn me about it?
The exe isn't code-signed — a certificate costs a few hundred a year — so Windows SmartScreen doesn't recognise it yet. The whole source is public on GitHub and every release is built from it. Click More info → Run anyway.
Do I need Intiface Central?
No. OscGoesPurrr carries its own Intiface engine and starts it for you. If you'd rather keep using Intiface Central, switch off Use built-in Intiface engine in Settings and it connects to Central instead.
Which toys work?
Anything Buttplug.io supports: Lovense, We-Vibe, Kiiroo, Satisfyer, Magic Motion and many more, linear strokers included. The IoST Index lists them all.
Which avatars work?
Any avatar with SPS / OGB contacts — the standard sockets, plugs and touch zones toy apps use. They're picked up automatically when you load the avatar. For anything unusual, SPS Sources builds a zone out of your avatar's own contact receivers.
Does it work with a Quest?
Yes, as long as VRChat runs on your PC — over Link, Air Link or Virtual Desktop. OscGoesPurrr runs on the same PC next to it.
Does it send anything over the internet?
Only one thing: at launch it asks GitHub whether there's a newer release, and you can switch that off in Settings. Everything else — VRChat, your toys, your statistics — stays on your PC and your local network.
Where are my settings?
In %APPDATA%\OscGoesPurrr\. A snapshot is taken at every launch (the newest five are kept) and can be restored from Settings.
What does it cost?
Nothing. It's free and open source under the MIT license.
Something isn't working — where do I get help?
Start with the app's own pages: OSC Diagnostics shows whether VRChat is reaching it and the System Log shows what happened. Still stuck? Open an issue on GitHub.
